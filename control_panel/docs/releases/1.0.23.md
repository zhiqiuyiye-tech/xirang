# Control Panel 1.0.23

Release validation date: 2026-10-21.

## Changes

Logical-volume grow and shrink now follow section 9 of the operator handbook
(`control_panel/docs/NFS创建流程.md`). Grow previously could not succeed on any
volume the panel serves.

- Grow runs online on a mounted logical volume, as the handbook's own command
  does: `lvextend -r -L +<delta>G`. Sections 5 to 7 mount every volume and export
  it over NFS while it is in use, and the resize preflight refused a mounted
  target for grow and shrink alike, so the only resizable volume was one nothing
  was using. A mounted, open or Pod-used volume is no longer a reason to refuse a
  grow.
- Grow completes the filesystem explicitly, as section 9 prescribes: `resize2fs`
  for ext2/ext3/ext4, and `xfs_growfs <mount target>` for a mounted XFS. An
  unmounted XFS needs no completion command, because `-r` resizes it through a
  temporary mount. A failed `lvextend` stops the step, so a filesystem-only
  success is never reported as a finished resize.
- XFS volumes can be grown. The flow refused XFS for grow and shrink alike,
  although XFS cannot only shrink. A shrink of an XFS volume is now refused with
  the reason (`XFS filesystem cannot be shrunk; grow it or recreate it`) instead
  of a generic unsupported-filesystem error.
- Shrink keeps the offline, ext-only boundary. It still refuses a mounted
  volume, a nonzero device-mapper open count, a swap-active volume and a Pod
  consumer, and a mounted volume now reports that host and holder namespaces must
  be unmounted first, which is the handbook's own 8.2 sequence.
- The resize guard pins the filesystem type in addition to the LV identity. Both
  the task preflight and the guarded SSH execution re-probe the filesystem and
  revalidate the device identity, so a filesystem change at the resize boundary
  stops the resize. A mounted XFS grow whose mount target disappeared is refused
  rather than resolved to another path.
- The preflight reads only the logical volume identity. It no longer runs the
  conservative namespace inspection, which also demanded proof that the volume's
  mounts propagate locally and refused the resize before any safety decision with
  `target/direct-parent propagation not proven local` (task #56 on worker 3). The
  identity read now uses the same identity-only operation the deletion flow
  relies on; the retired inspection and its operation were removed. A volume
  mounted at an operating-system path or a reserved mount point is still refused,
  now by validating the live mount targets the safety probe already reads.
- The preflight reports what it approved: a grow step says which filesystem it is
  growing online, and a shrink step still says the volume is unused and
  unprotected.

## Validation and operational limitations

- Full Go tests, `go vet ./...`, and `go build ./...`.
- 70 Python parser/physical-protection tests, plus Ruff check and format checks.
- 60 frontend/deployment tests, including UI/Helm version consistency.
- `helm lint`, Go format, JavaScript syntax and whitespace checks.

Tests use a scripted SSH runner and mocked Kubernetes access. No real worker
volume was grown or shrunk. Online grow depends on the worker's LVM, e2fsprogs
and xfsprogs behaviour and on the filesystem being mounted in the namespace where
the SSH command runs, so isolated Linux acceptance on a real worker is still
required; the resize commands themselves are never exercised on a mounted volume
by the local suites. A mounted grow does not re-read the capacity on NFS clients:
a client that cached the share size sees the new size after remounting, which is
an operational step and not a panel guarantee.

The 2026-10-10 storage operation safety design refused a mounted target for grow
and shrink alike and stated that online growth was not exempt. Section 9 of the
handbook supersedes that rule for grow, and the design document carries the
2026-10-21 amendment that records why, together with a pointer in its
implementation plan. Shrink is unchanged from that design. The namespace-recovery
guide now states that the resize flow follows handbook section 9 rather than the
stricter boundary.

UI, Helm chart version and appVersion are 1.0.23. The independent browser-crypto
build tooling remains at 0.1.0. This source release does not publish an image or
OCI chart, or deploy to production. Development database and local plan files
are excluded from the release commit.
